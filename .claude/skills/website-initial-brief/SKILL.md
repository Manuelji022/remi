# Website Initial Brief

Help the user define the identity and direction of a website through a thoughtful interview. The final output must be useful to both visual designers creating a mood board and frontend developers implementing the experience. Do not merely transcribe answers or produce a generic design summary: synthesize them into a distinctive, reasoned creative vision.

## Interview approach

- Start by explaining that you will ask questions in manageable groups and can accept rough notes, links, or "I don't know yet" answers.
- Ask one question at a time (or a small, clearly grouped set when tightly related). Adapt follow-ups to the answers; avoid asking for information already provided.
- Work through these areas, covering all relevant questions from the source brief below: organization and offer; business context and positioning; audience and accessibility; discovery channels, competitors and inspiration; current website strengths/issues and analytics; project goals, user experience, CTA and desired feelings; pages, languages, domain, hosting, functionality, CMS, integrations and schedule; other constraints or context.
- Ask for concrete evidence and examples where useful. For inspirations, ask what specifically resonates (composition, imagery, tone, interaction, color, etc.), not just which sites they like.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, preferences, hypotheses, and open decisions. Never invent brand facts or present assumptions as confirmed.
- When enough information is gathered, briefly recap the interpretation and ask the user to correct or approve it before writing the final brief. If the user wants to stop early, produce a draft and clearly label gaps.

## Creative synthesis

Develop a central creative concept that translates the business, audience, and goals into a memorable visual/experiential metaphor—e.g. mountain, balance, magnetism, or another concept that genuinely fits the answers. Do not choose a metaphor just because it sounds poetic. Explain the connection to the brand and user needs.

The concept should give designers a starting point for mood-board exploration, not dictate a finished design system. Describe how it could influence visual language (imagery, color atmosphere, typography, composition, materials/textures, motion, interaction, and pacing) while leaving room for exploration. Include a few keywords and a short list of things to avoid. Where useful, offer two or three alternative concept territories and recommend one, explaining the trade-offs. Avoid cliché adjectives and unsupported prescriptions.

## Final deliverable

Create a polished Markdown document named `website-initial-brief.md` in the current project. If the appropriate destination is unclear, ask where to save it. The document should be self-contained, easy to share with designers and frontend developers, and written in the user's preferred language (otherwise use the language of the conversation). Include:

1. Project snapshot (client/project/date if known).
2. Organization, mission, positioning, products/services, differentiator.
3. Audience, needs, accessibility, devices and discovery context.
4. Competitive landscape, inspirations and current-site findings.
5. Goals, desired experience, CTA and intended user feelings.
6. **Creative direction**: concept name; concise vision statement; rationale; keywords; visual/experiential expression for imagery, palette, typography, layout/composition, motion/interactions and tone; mood-board prompts; avoid-list. Mark proposed directions as proposals, not settled brand decisions.
7. Information architecture and content/pages.
8. Functional, technical, CMS, integration and hosting requirements; languages/domain.
9. Timeline, constraints and deliverables.
10. Open questions, assumptions and decisions requiring approval.

Use concise, specific prose. Preserve meaningful user language where appropriate, but rewrite it for clarity. Make requirements actionable for frontend without prematurely choosing a technology stack unless the user has specified one. Do not claim to generate or attach a mood board; provide a creative brief that enables one to be developed.

## Source questionnaire

Use these prompts as coverage guidance, not as a rigid form to read verbatim:

### About
- General presentation and business sector: who are you?
- Organization/company mission: why?
- Positioning: how?
- Products and/or services: what?

### Context
- Target audience (age, profile, interests, etc.).
- Devices used (desktop, laptop, tablet, mobile).
- How users find the website (social media, campaigns, word of mouth).
- Accessibility needs.
- Competitors and their websites.
- Differentiating factor.
- Inspiring websites/brands and why they inspire you.
- Current-site analytics, if available.
- What works and does not work on the current site?

### Goals
- Project expectations and goals.
- Desired user experience.
- Main call to action / key action or section.
- Desired look and feel and what users should feel while browsing.

### Deliverables and technical information
- Expected pages.
- Website languages and domain.
- Hosting/server and whether a solution or access is needed.
- Specific functionality (filters, newsletter, calendar, search, map, forms, etc.).
- Whether a CMS is needed; preference and documentation/training needs.
- APIs, websites, or apps to connect.
- Schedule and fixed deadline.
- Any additional relevant information.
